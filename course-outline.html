Course description

What. This course builds practical fluency in Generative AI and agentic AI systems for management consulting. Students will learn how large language models (LLMs) work, become fluent users of tools such as Claude and ChatGPT, master prompt engineering, and progress to designing, building, and evaluating multi-purpose AI agents. These agent skills are then applied to core consulting deliverables such as market and competitive research, financial models and quant analysis, data science and insight generation, and C-suite communication, culminating in an end-to-end capstone consulting engagement.

Why. Generative AI and autonomous agents are rapidly reshaping how consulting and analytical work gets done, from research and synthesis to modelling and client-ready deliverables. Future consultants and managers must be able to work fluently and critically with these tools: knowing what agents can and cannot do, how to direct them reliably, and how to responsibly manage their risks and failure modes, so they can multiply their own judgement and productivity rather than be replaced by, or over-rely on, the technology.

How. Through a build-as-you-learn pedagogy: concept sessions on LLMs and agent architecture, hands-on labs (prompt engineering, agent tools, MCP, agent skills), guided design-and-build of a personal multi-purpose agent, applied consulting labs (research, financial modelling, data analytics, insight and C-suite output generation), peer critique and iteration, and a two-session capstone that simulates an end-to-end consulting engagement using AI agents, closing with a critical look at failure cases, risks, and the future of AI-powered consulting.

Pedagogy

Delivered primarily through hands-on, in-classroom build sessions rather than lectures. The instructor live-builds each technique or agent workflow step by step, while students replicate it in real time, progressing from guided replication to independently applying it to their own consulting deliverables, culminating in the two-part Capstone, where students build and stress-test a working agent end-to-end.

Academic integrity and course etiquette
  • Electronics Policy: Laptops open and required every session. Phones and non-course apps should stay closed during active build/discussion time.
  • Academic Integrity: This course is a deliberate exception to MICA’s default no-AI-tools policy. Full use of Claude, ChatGPT, and agentic tools is required and expected. The only requirements are transparency (note which tools/agents you used for each deliverable) and personal accountability (you are responsible for verifying and standing behind anything an AI produced). Standard integrity rules still apply to non-AI matters.
  • Collaboration Guidelines: Peer collaboration is encouraged during in-class labs and the Capstone (group work). Quiz and individual reflections must be your own.
  • All work you submit for this course must be original. If you do borrow from another source for any purpose, please follow APA guidelines. If you are not sure what plagiarism means, consult your Student Manual or your faculty.
  • Additionally, the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools (such as ChatGPT, Quillbot or other AI text generators) to create or significantly produce content that you submit as your own original work is strictly prohibited and constitutes plagiarism, unless otherwise directed/approved by the concerned course instructor.
  • Any violation of academic integrity through plagiarism, fabrication, or other unfair practices will be subject to disciplinary action as outlined in MICA's Code of Academic Integrity.
  • All examination protocols and disciplines detailed in the Student Manual are sacrosanct; any violation will be subject to disciplinary action as outlined in MICA's Code of Academic Integrity.

Introduction to LLMs, GenAI and understanding model capabilities

Pre-read. Andrej Karpathy, "Intro to Large Language Models" (1-hour talk) — a jargon-light mental model of what LLMs are, how they are trained, and what they can and cannot do. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zjkBMFhNj_g

In Class. Discussion Questions
Q1. Where have you already seen generative AI change how analytical or knowledge work gets done, and what surprised you most about its capabilities or its limits?
Q2. If an LLM is essentially predicting the next token, why can it still summarise a report or draft an email? What does that tell us about where it will be reliable versus risky?

Post Read/Reflections. Write a one-page reflection listing three consulting tasks you believe an LLM could do well today and three it would likely fail at, with your reasoning for each.

In Class. Discussion Questions
Q1. In your own words, what does “attention” let a model do that older approaches could not? Use a business-writing example.
Q2. Knowing that a model is trained on a fixed dataset up to a cut-off date, how should that shape the way a consultant fact-checks its output?

Post Read/Reflections. Explain the transformer’s core idea to a non-technical colleague in under 200 words and submit your explanation.

In Class. Discussion Questions
Q1. What separates a novice user of Claude/ChatGPT from a fluent one? Share one prompt that worked well and one that failed for you.
Q2. When should you prefer a chat model over a traditional tool (search engine, spreadsheet, calculator), and when should you not?

Post Read/Reflections. Use Claude and ChatGPT on the same real task, then write a short comparison of where each was stronger, weaker, or simply different.

SESSION 4 · 4 August 2026 · Ketan Paithankar

Fundamentals of Prompt Engineering

Pre-read. DAIR.AI, "Prompt Engineering Guide" — a structured open reference covering core prompting techniques with worked examples. https://www.promptingguide.ai/

In Class. Discussion Questions
Q1. Which prompting technique (role, few-shot examples, step-by-step, output format) most improved your results, and why do you think it helped?
Q2. How much of “prompt engineering” is about the model versus about clearly specifying the task the way you would brief a junior analyst?

Post Read/Reflections. Take a weak prompt and iterate it through at least three versions; submit all versions with notes on what changed and why the output improved.

By the end of Module 1, students will be able to:

  • Explain, in plain language, what LLMs and generative AI are, how they are trained, and the core idea of the transformer architecture that powers them.
  • Describe the capabilities and limitations of current models, including why they can be confidently wrong and how a knowledge cut-off affects reliability.
  • Operate Claude and ChatGPT fluently for common analytical and writing tasks and select the appropriate tool for a given job.
  • Apply core prompt-engineering techniques (role, examples, step-by-step reasoning, output format) and iterate a prompt to measurably improve its output.

The Future of AI-Powered Consulting

Pre-read. Ethan Mollick, "One Useful Thing" — ongoing, grounded writing on where AI is taking knowledge work and expertise. https://www.oneusefulthing.org/

In Class. Discussion Questions
Q1. If agents can do more of the analytical grunt work, what becomes the distinctive value a human consultant offers?
Q2. What skills should you build now to stay valuable as these tools keep improving?

Post Read/Reflections. Write a personal one-page manifesto: how you intend to use AI agents in your career while keeping your judgement and integrity central.

By the end of Module 6, students will be able to:

  • Advise how an organisation can move from AI pilots to measurable value by redesigning workflows around agents.
  • Analyse real-world agent failure modes and risks (e.g. goal hijacking, memory poisoning, prompt injection, data leakage) and propose guardrails to mitigate them.
  • Draft a responsible-use policy for AI agents in a consulting context.
  • Articulate the enduring, distinctive value of human judgement and the skills to build for a career in AI-powered consulting.
Recommended texts and additional reading
  • Mollick, Ethan. Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI. Portfolio/Penguin, 2024.
  • Davenport, T. & Mittal, N. All-in on AI: How Smart Companies Win Big with Artificial Intelligence. Harvard Business Review Press, 2023.
  • McKinsey/BCG “State of AI” annual report series (updated through the term) — for current adoption data.
  • Anthropic and OpenAI official documentation: Claude / Model Context Protocol (MCP) specification; ChatGPT / GPT platform documentation (updated through the term).
