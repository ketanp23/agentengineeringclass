Section 01 · Concept

What is an AI Agent?

An AI Agent is an AI system that goes beyond generating replies: it uses a large language model as its reasoning engine to pursue a goal autonomously — perceiving its context, planning multi-step actions, using tools and knowledge sources, and adapting based on the results — all while operating within defined instructions and guardrails.

Chatbot vs. Agent: a chatbot answers one message at a time from what it already knows. An agent decides what to do next — it can identify who it's talking to, choose the right skill, search a knowledge base, filter what it retrieves, and escalate when it can't help. Same conversation surface, fundamentally more capable behavior.

Characteristics

What makes it an agent, not just a chatbot

🎯

Goal-driven

Works toward an outcome — "resolve this driver's question" — not just producing a plausible next message.

🧭

Autonomous

Decides its own next step: ask a clarifying question, search, route to a skill, or escalate to a human.

🛠️

Tool & skill use

Invokes capabilities beyond text generation — knowledge-base search, playbooks, and external actions.

📚

Grounded in knowledge

Answers from retrieved, curated sources rather than general model knowledge — reducing hallucination.

🧩

Context-aware

Tracks who the user is (driver vs. courier) and carries that context through the whole conversation.

🔒

Bounded by guardrails

Operates within explicit rules — never mixing driver and courier policies, never guessing pay rates.

🔁

Multi-step reasoning

Chains identify → route → retrieve → answer, walking users through processes one step at a time.

🤝

Knows its limits

When no relevant information is found, it says so and hands off to the human support team.

Section 02 · Hands-on Activity

Build the GoRoute Customer Support Agent

GoRoute is a fictitious ride-sharing and delivery platform. In this lab you will use voiceflow.com to build a support agent that answers Driver and Courier queries — each grounded in its own knowledge base, kept separate by metadata filtering, and routed through dedicated playbook skills.

What you will build
  • A support agent with a global prompt defining its personality, tone, and guardrails (never mix driver and courier policies; never guess pay rates).
  • A knowledge base loaded from the GoRoute Help Center, tagged with metadata (user_type = driver / courier).
  • Two playbook skills — Driver Support and Courier Support — that the agent routes to based on who the user is.
  • Metadata filtering so each playbook retrieves only its own user type's content.

Section 03 · Debrief Activity

Your Debrief — 3 Simple Parts

Building the agent is only half the learning. This short personal reflection helps you put the experience into your own words — no technical jargon needed. Download the worksheet, find a quiet moment, and write plainly and honestly. There are no right answers, only your answers.

The 3 parts
Part 1
What did I just build? Explain your helper in one or two simple sentences — like you would to a friend — and why it was smart of it to ask “driver or courier?” before answering.
Part 2
What made it smart (and safe)? Two easy “what if” questions: what could go wrong if driver and courier information got mixed up, and why the helper only answers from the GoRoute help pages.
Part 3
My key learnings. Your top 3 learnings from the session, one idea for where a helper like this could be useful in your own work or life, and one thing you're still curious about.
